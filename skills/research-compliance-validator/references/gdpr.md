# GDPR Checklist for Research Projects

Reference: Regulation (EU) 2016/679

## Lawful Basis & Purpose (Art. 5, 6)
- [ ] **Lawful basis identified**: A valid legal basis for processing personal data is documented (consent, legitimate interest, legal obligation, etc.)
- [ ] **Purpose limitation**: Data is collected for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes and not processed incompatibly
- [ ] **Data minimisation**: Only data necessary for the research purpose is collected
- [ ] **Storage limitation**: Retention periods are defined and data is deleted/anonymised after the research period

## Special Category Data (Art. 9)
- [ ] **Special category data identified**: Health, genetic, biometric, race/ethnicity, political opinions, religious beliefs data is explicitly identified if present
- [ ] **Additional safeguard for special categories**: Explicit consent or other Art. 9(2) basis documented for special category data
- [ ] **Pseudonymisation / anonymisation applied**: Special category data is pseudonymised or anonymised where possible

## Consent (Art. 7)
- [ ] **Informed consent obtained**: Participants are clearly informed about what data is collected and why
- [ ] **Consent freely given**: No coercion or imbalance of power; withdrawal is as easy as giving consent
- [ ] **Consent records kept**: Evidence of consent is maintained
- [ ] **Right to withdraw**: Participants can withdraw consent and their data is removed upon withdrawal

## Transparency & Information (Art. 13)
- [ ] **Privacy notice provided**: Data subjects receive information about controller identity, purposes, legal basis, retention, rights
- [ ] **Plain language used**: Privacy information is clear and understandable to participants
- [ ] **Contact details for DPO/controller provided**: Participants know who to contact with questions

## Data Subject Rights (Art. 17, 20, 21)
- [ ] **Right to erasure process defined**: A process exists to delete a participant's data upon request
- [ ] **Right to access process defined**: Participants can obtain a copy of their data
- [ ] **Right to object addressed**: Participants can object to processing; process documented

## Data Protection by Design (Art. 25)
- [ ] **Privacy by design applied**: Privacy considerations are built into the research design from the start, not added later
- [ ] **Default settings are privacy-protective**: Systems default to minimum data collection
- [ ] **Access controls in place**: Only authorised researchers can access personal data

## Records of Processing (Art. 30)
- [ ] **Processing activity documented**: A record exists describing what data is processed, for what purpose, who has access, where stored

## Data Protection Impact Assessment (Art. 35)
- [ ] **DPIA conducted if high-risk**: A DPIA is completed for processing likely to result in high risk (large scale special category data, systematic monitoring, automated decision-making)
- [ ] **DPIA covers risks and mitigations**: The DPIA identifies risks and documents how they are mitigated

## International Transfers
- [ ] **Transfers outside EU/EEA addressed**: If data is transferred outside EU/EEA, appropriate safeguards are in place (adequacy decision, SCCs, BCRs)

## Scoring guidance
- Pass: Clear documented evidence in project files
- Partial: Mentioned but incomplete or unclear
- Fail: No evidence found or clear violation
- N/A: Not applicable (e.g., no personal data collected)
